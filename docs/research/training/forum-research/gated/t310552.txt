--- 310552.4 | ? | 7/2/2021 5:03:19 PM ---
It's exponential. Imagine an S shaped curve with +0 being the center. The difference between +0 and +1 in skills is not that big but the differnce between +5 and +6 already a lot more. Once you get to +10 levels you'll sometimes see double pops (players ging from pitiful to inept, skipping awful for example).

Source: We used to have an old external site that gathered data on elasticity and it modeled this curve.

I can see that you are a potential customer for my decades-old free tool, the coachParrot excel sheet, see (119279.1) and other threads linked from there.

You can use the 'Scout' tab of the coachParrot tool to try to answer this sort of questions of how to develop a player. You can experiment with target skill profiles and set priorities for the training of each skill. The tool will then generate a training plan that approaches your target, and by varying the skill priorities you can check how long it takes to reach the target with different priority settings, or how close you can get if the target is not quite achievable.

coachParrot includes a model of the elastic effect that is probably quite close to the truth, so as a learning tool it has its merits. Just take everything with a pinch of salt, since most of the formulas are off by a few percentage points, because we never collected enough data to fix the model perfectly. The best thing we ever had before everybody went lazy were some 30,000 weekly training results collected by the US NT community. Good luck!

I'm also a fan of SB (right now, my guys are (ID-SB) 17-19 and 17-20, with both of them probably going one skill up in the future, hopefully for both skills; bench is 12-16 and 14-14, with the latter guy being a primary bench big who'll end up somewhere in the 16-18 range).

The thing with SB is that it necessitates slashing another skill due to cap reasons; my choice was IS since I always wanted to run an outside-oriented team. Salary-wise, it's very affordable with not so great JS (starters' salaries are 74k and 95k respectively, with 15 and 16 RB and very low IS), but you can go for higher JS and consequently higher salaries, depending on the potential, though my option would be to stack them with PA instead and have 1-3 positions as primary offensive guys. Of course, you can also adapt this overall plan to your level of competition and not go for skills as high as possible like I did. However, I think that 95k for a very good defensive big with some JS and PA is not that much of a problem (or 75k for 17-20 with less shooting). People still force inside tactics predominantly, and they're in for a nice surprise that way. And just to boast a little bit, I'm pretty sure my guy posted the best blk/game average in my first league ever last season, with a nice and round 4.0. SB works if you know how to use it.

The effect of SB is noticeable and positive for your team, especially if you construct it so as to be able to play 3-2 zone (RB pretty much all round, though not necessarily very high on 1-3 pos).
