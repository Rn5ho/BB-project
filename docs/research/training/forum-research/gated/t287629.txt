--- 287629.2 | ? | 6/23/2017 1:02:39 AM ---
Imo you need to look at the bigger picture. What do you want your players to look like when he's done? How much can fit on his potential? What are his current skill set?
After deciding what you want him to look like when done you know what you got to work with. Then I would suggest to start evening up any gap in his skills he has so that no skill is left behind.
Once that's done you can set up a elastic effect if you want.
Get HA and DR 2-4 pops higher then the other skills and then catch up with the other.
Even though you see a really positive effect when training OD and PA for example with high HA and DR i don't think you win anything by doing so in a extreme way. You'll only lose speed on the 1on1 training instead. Only that you won't notice it so much since it's a fast training and you have a young player.

--- 287629.3 | ? | 6/23/2017 11:20:22 AM ---
Elasticity has 2 sides...negative and positive. If the skills are too disproportionate to other related skills it can slow down the training of the highest skill. If i were to make a recommendation, not more than 5+ skill points. Just to keep enjoying the positive elasticity. Handling and Driving are the most over trained skill as a rookie. Being a former u21 mentor (almost 8 seasons) this is my opinion having experienced the negative aspect of elasticity.

--- 287629.4 | LA-zajino | 6/23/2017 12:15:41 PM ---
My opinion is to pull DR up to level 14-15 no matter what other skills are first (that means first 2 seasons). This way you can experience double pops even in 21-22 yo players. I had DR17 while JR2 and OD3. Next week the speed of JR training was about 1,6 level (made pop from 2 to 4). You can make 5-6 JR/OD pops in 8-9 weeks even in 21 years. I go for driving first (when you dont mind high DR player). It looked i could save up to 6 weeks of training this way in 10 seasons. Not done yet but I strongly believe in it.
