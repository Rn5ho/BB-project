 Hi Marin,

Thank you for taking the time to answer the community's questions and for continuing to engage with us on these updates.

As a newer manager stepping into this game, I’ve spent a lot of time reading the history on the forums. I am honestly so grateful and humbled to just walk in the lanes that this incredible community has spent decades paving and building. But looking at how things are being handled right now leaves me feeling genuinely sad for them and sad overall.

It feels a bit like a person making every single payment on a car for years, treating it like their own, only to have someone sneak up in the dead of night, take the keys back because "you didn't actually pay for it," and then tell them to "find a way to work." You hand out the keys and the sandbox to build these rosters, but without a full blueprint for every player type, the community had to reverse-engineer the math through sheer dedication no loop holes even if the player’s are kinda wacky. We all have to understand nothing by chance nothing is pure,. But To watch managers pour years of their lives into building amazing athletes, only for you to take the keys, wipe the drawing boards clean, and say "No, that's illegal/unrealistic, /abuse of code”—it just feels wrong.

Also, on the point of realism: given how long this game has been around, evolution is natural. Over time, evolution creates more refined, beautiful, and better versions of things. A lot of folks might say this new evolution of player costs less in some areas while delivering more overall value—and honestly, I don't see how a better, more evolved version of the game is a bad thing overall! But calling those master-crafted player builds "unrealistic" just doesn't sit right when that progression is the natural result of struggle throughout the years of game age minds within the community and evolution of it.

I understand you missed or skipped a couple of the tougher tactical questions, but that's okay—the under-the-hood math is deeply complex.

Thank you again for your time, your patience, and for keeping the game moving forward. It's a wonderful world to step into, even if the garage feels a little colder tonight.

 Hi Marin,

I know this is supposed to be a Q&A, but I'm not going to ask any questions. Instead, what follows is my counterargument to the reasons you've given for each change you made to the salary formula, as well as to the way you implemented those changes. I'll try to explain my perspective on the issue and why I believe you've created even more chaos.

First of all, you wrote that users don't like changes. I won't speak for everyone, but personally I do like changes, as long as they are made carefully, with a clear objective and a well-thought-out plan designed to achieve that objective.

I'd also like to remind you that, at least on this particular issue, a large part of the BB community had been asking for changes to the salary formula for a long time. So in this case, the argument that "users hate changes" has no real basis. I mention this because I hope you don't believe that the reactions to the new salary formula are simply the result of people hating change.

You said that one of your goals was to encourage managers to stop training players earlier, with higher salaries acting as the incentive. I don't understand why players should have to stop training earlier in the first place, or why this is something that needs to be "fixed" in BB. But let's assume for the sake of discussion that it is a change in the right direction. Why didn't you simply reduce the overall cap for newly drafted players, or reduce the number of Hall of Fame prospects appearing in each draft? That would have achieved your goal much more naturally and gradually over the course of 10-15 seasons. Instead, in order to compensate for the problems created by your change, you're now increasing the salary cap, which will ultimately produce exactly the opposite result from what you originally wanted to achieve.

From what I understand, another one of your goals was to limit teams that accumulate cash and build extremely expensive rosters while consistently spending far more than they earn. I don't understand how increasing the salaries of elite players accomplishes that goal. If a team wants to operate at a 200k weekly loss, it will still be able to do so, it will simply do it with different players. This isn't a measure that prevents that behavior. The only thing you've accomplished is temporarily pushing those teams to a 400k loss. Within one or two seasons they'll adapt, and they'll be right back to operating at a 200k loss.

You also said that salary increases mainly affected players earning over $180k per week, while players with lower salaries generally saw reductions. According to you, this will encourage managers to build larger rosters. Do you honestly believe this is enough incentive for someone to build a roster with eight or more players? Didn't you think that teams would simply have more room in their payroll to absorb the salary increases of the players you raised their salaries? Or that they might simply choose to save the extra money instead?

You also mention that the salary increase for teams with the highest payrolls is only a single digit percentage, in fact a very low one. Obviously you have access to much more complete data than I do, but what I'm seeing in the Hellas A1 division is very close to 10%. We're talking about an increase of 50k-100k per team. That's extremely difficult for anyone to absorb overnight. But even that is just a drop in the ocean.

 Further down you write, "ID is an important skill and is valued as such." I don't want to get into how important OD is, so let's assume the two skills are equally important. From a salary perspective, it's relatively cheap to have a player with 20+ OD. On the other hand, it has always been very expensive to have a player with 20+ ID. With your changes, ID has become even more expensive. Do a quick analysis and see how many players in BB have 20+ OD compared to how many have 20+ ID. Even PFs and Cs rarely reach 20 ID because it's simply too expensive. The only players who consistently have high ID are SB bigs, and after the changes you implemented, they will continue to be the only ones.

On top of increasing the cost of ID for SFs, you also increased the cost of SB. Obviously, the only way to develop elite ID is to train it, and training ID also increases SB. In other words, you're effectively penalizing ID training twice, pushing managers away from developing SFs. Honestly, the new formula is an SF killer. There is simply no reason to have an SF on your team anymore.

You then say, "we are making all these changes to try and prevent illogical lineups (like shot blockers on 1 and 2, etc)." First of all, who exactly is "we"? I haven't heard the opinion of any other BB besides yours. I'd genuinely like to know if anyone else was involved in what has turned into this parody.

Second, this time I can actually say that I agree with your reasoning to some extent. If one of your goals is to discourage shot blockers from playing at the 1 and 2, why didn't you increase the salary impact of PA, and perhaps HA, for those players? That would have made them less efficient in those positions.

Likewise, why wasn't the salary cost of PA reduced significantly for PGs (if it was reduced at all), so that we could actually build true point guards to play at the 1? For 72 seasons it has been practically impossible to have an elite PG with 17+ Passing. Isn't that a loophole that deserves to be addressed? Wouldn't that kind of change actually help managers use players in their "natural" positions, which you said is one of your goals?

Also, if that really is the goal, then the cost of IS should have been reduced even more for bigs and increased more for guards. Despite the changes you made, it is still relatively affordable to have guards with extremely high IS (21+). Meanwhile, having 21 IS on a big is economically inefficient, and reaching 22 is almost impossible unless the player has a major weakness in ID or RB.

The only thing I'm trying to explain with this post is that the changes you made will achieve almost none of the goals you set out to accomplish. They were an impulsive decision, implemented without any real analysis of the consequences. In fact, in several areas you will end up achieving exactly the opposite of what you intended.

The increase in the cost of ID is especially disastrous. It will encourage teams to train only IS, and naturally, if you don't invest in ID, the best defensive tactic will be the 3-2 zone. The 3-2 works best with SB bigs, so they will continue to be essential.

At the same time, inside oriented offenses work best when you have extremely high IS at the 3-4-5 positions. As a result, high IS guards will simply play there, while the SB bigs will end up playing exactly where you didn't wanted, 1 and 2.

Well done!
