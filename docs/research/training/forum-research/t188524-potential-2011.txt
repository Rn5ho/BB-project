Formula for Potential Caps
(see this thread below for discussion; this message has final results based on a research using 2276 player samples)
To calculate whether a player has reached his potential cap, calculate the maximum of his five positional 'Potential Scores', where each positional score is defined by the sum of multiplying the player's skill with the weights given below. Since these weight coefficients are different from the salary formula (that determines the position displayed on the player page), it can happen that a player's maximum Potential Score is from a neighbouring position role, for example a C is sometimes capped by the potential-formula for PFs.
PU(X)=max(PU_PG(X),PU_SG(X),PU_SF(X),PU_PF(X),PU_C(X))
For example, PU_PG(X) would be 0.18*JS(X)+0.26*JR(X)+...+0.03*SB(X)
JS JR OD HA DR PA IS ID RB SB
PG ,18 ,26 ,30 ,24 ,12 ,52 ,03 ,04 ,20 ,03
SG ,45 ,50 ,42 ,05 ,04 ,08 ,03 ,05 ,25 ,03
SF ,58 ,34 ,26 ,05 ,03 ,03 ,05 ,25 ,33 ,03
PF ,32 ,06 ,07 ,05 ,03 ,02 ,40 ,40 ,40 ,20
C ,06 ,08 ,01 ,04 ,03 ,01 ,46 ,46 ,46 ,25
Potential has sublevels. For each level of potential, the Cap is a Potential Score-value in the range as stated below. You do not know beforehand where in the range your player will cap, that is a question of luck. Sublevels of skills influence the calculation as well. For example, if your player has potential 7 perennial allstar, he will reach his cap when he reaches a Potential Score somewhere between 22 and 24. From that moment on, his training will be 3 times slower than before (this is an estimate). The transition is gradual, the training will become progressively slower as the player gets very close to his cap and crosses the threshold - it is not understood yet how this slowdown progresses exactly.
Based on anecdotal evidence it is believed that potential 0 announcer is just the minimum sublevel of bench-warmer, and that potential 11 all-time great is the maximum sublevel of hall of famer. Even at the highest levels of potential, there are a few players in the game that have clearly reached their Cap.
Caps:
0 announcer: 10
1 bench-warmer: 10 - 12
2 role player: 12 - 14
3 6th man: 14 - 16
4 starter: 16 - 18
5 star: 18 - 20
6 allstar: 20 - 22
7 perennial allstar: 22 - 24
8 superstar: 24 - 26
9 MVP: 26 - 28
10 hall of famer: 28 - 30
11 all-time great: 30

So over the last few days the Parrot on my shoulder got curious about potential, after reading some ideas floating around in the forums, mostly in this thread (187979.10) . I started collecting some data from the transfer list for playing around, then yesterday on the bus I had an idea for how to drill down on this and after a few hours of fiddling last night, to my surprise there was the structure of the formula, with some workable estimates for all coefficients, based on a grand total of c. 135 player samples. Because it is so little data there are still some artefacts in the coefficients which will go away once we have about three times as much data – for which I’ll request your help in a moment. But to keep up the bragging-momentum, here’s the story first:
At any given moment there are about 10000 players on the transfer list, distributed over 10 levels of potential and 5 nominal positions, so that makes 200 at each position/potential combination. It is likely that at least the “best” few of each 200-group are capped at a high sublevel of the respective potential-level, in particular since noticing that one’s player is capped (often at a low level of potential, don’t think MVP/hall of famer here!) is an incentive to sell. Of course initially we don’t know what “best” means for purposes of potential, it could be more like sum of skills or more like salary, but only one of these two - salary - is searchable, and you would assume that in any case also among the players with the highest salary, some are capped, so this is the easy route: search at every level of potential, and every position, for a few players with the highest salaries, then record all their skills. Don’t necessarily search for the ones with the maximum salary, because they can be outliers that have been trained far beyond the cap, especially at lower levels of potential, but search for a leading group that is close together – then the extreme outliers can be eliminated, and one ends up with players that are at or minimally above the cap, at a high sublevel of their potential (notice that this is an advantage over HeadPaperPusher’s previous method, which could not distinguish sublevels).
Initially these are very few data points at each position/potential combination, but this is where my idea from the bus comes in: As has been known for a while, and as quick look at such data confirms, both the salary and the skillsum perspective reveal differences depending on position: at all levels of potential, capped guards almost always have higher salaries than capped SFs, and higher sums of skills than capped Bigs. Capped PFs have lower salaries but higher sums of skills than capped Cs. But potential should not look like this; what you want (as a game designer) is a single value that defines a level of potential; for example “(max sublevel of) bench warmer = max. 6”, independent of position. So then there is a big constraint: whatever the Potential formula is, it has to equalize those differences between the cap-levels for the five positions, and it has to do so at each of the 10 levels of potential – and since this equalization has to happen for all players in the database simultaneously, we now can use the whole dataset to determine the coefficients – suddenly we have much more training data to determine the coefficients than we thought we did!

It turned out that the formula is again position dependent, there are no good results when searching for a formula for all positions simultaneously (sorry Crazy!) So we are looking for 5x10=50 coefficients. 135 data points are the bare minimum to get results on that fitting task, but still with artefacts, so we really want 400 before the coefficients are stable enough to be published. But what I can say already after achieving very good match on the data is this:
A) Potential is a position-dependent linear combination involving the ten skills
B) The weights in the Potential formula are different from both salary and team rating weights – they are somehow in between, with an interesting feature (hope it stays like this when more data come in):
C) Offensive skills are weighted more heavily in the Potential formula than defensive skills; you need relatively higher potential to train a franchise leader that shines on offense. If a player has an issue with potential, then a more defensive profile may be more suitable for him – I love this :-)
D) Since the Potential formula is different from the salary formula, it happens that players get capped by the limit on a neighbouring position role.
E) Potential levels are spaced like this:
0 announcer: cap 5 [in potential-units]
1 bench-warmer: 5 (cap at minimum sublevel of potential) to 6 (cap at maximum sublevel)
2 role player: 6 to 7
3 6th man: 7 to 8
4 starter: 8 to 9
5 star: 9 to 9.75
6 allstar: 9.75 to 10.5
7 perennial allstar: 10.5 to 11.25
8 superstar: 11.25 to 12
9 MVP: 12 to 13
10 hall of famer: 13 to 14
11 all-time great: 14
The four middle levels of potentials, which are shared by the largest number of players, are more closely spaced than the more infrequent levels at the extremes; announcer is simply minimum sublevel of bench-warmer, and all-time great is maximum sublevel of hall of famer; I believe there are two capped all-time greats in the game and several capped hall of famers. Salaries much above what we are seeing now at the top end are highly unlikely also for the future.
